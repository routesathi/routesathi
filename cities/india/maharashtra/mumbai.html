FIRST, CHOOSE YOUR PACE

How much Mumbai do you really have time for?

A first day should stay in South Mumbai. A second day lets you visit Elephanta Caves properly. Use a third day for one different side of the city—Bandra, a museum or a slower sea-facing evening—not all three.

YOUR READY ROUTE

Start by the harbour.
End by the sea.

Maharashtra Tourism lists Gateway of India, Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus, Marine Drive and Elephanta among Mumbai's core experiences. This guide groups them by travel direction so the day remains practical.

South Mumbai heritage and a sea evening

Use this as the first Mumbai route. These are sensible planning times, not guaranteed entry slots—leave the traffic buffer intact and check any paid attraction before going. Elephanta is deliberately not included here.

PLAN B · TWO DAYS

South Mumbai first, Elephanta next

Day 1 follows the timed South Mumbai route. Day 2 is for Elephanta Caves and its ferry journey from the Gateway. Maharashtra Tourism says the ferry takes roughly an hour each way and the site is closed on Mondays; treat these as a planning schedule and verify the live ferry and on-site rules first.

PLAN C · EXTRA TIME

Choose one more Mumbai mood

A third day should make the trip easier, not stretch it. Pick one: a museum near the harbour, a Bandra sea walk, or a slower food-and-café day. Do not make one day jump from South Mumbai to every beach-side stop.

3 daysSlow city plan

DAY 2 · 08:00–16:30

Elephanta day

08:00 ferry check, around 10:15 caves, then a 14:30 return-ferry target with a flexible evening.

DAY 3 · START 10:30

One different city side

Use Bandra, a museum or a calm local day. Keep a 1:30 PM lunch and 5:30 PM sea walk on the same side.

Why extra time helps:Mumbai becomes enjoyable when each day has one direction and enough buffer for traffic, queues and a proper meal.

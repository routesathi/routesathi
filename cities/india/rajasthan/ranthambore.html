PLAN B · TWO DAYS

Two safari chances, done properly

This is the RouteSathi recommendation. Two separate drives mean two different light conditions and a more honest wildlife experience. It still does not guarantee a tiger sighting—and that is exactly how a responsible safari should be presented.

2 safari drivesRecommended plan

DAY 1 · Arrive + afternoon safari

  1. 11:30Arrive and check inChoose a stay that can get you to the required reporting point on time.
  2. 12:30Lunch + permit and ID checkConfirm every detail in writing before leaving the hotel.
  3. AfternoonSafari drive 1Let the forest decide the experience; enjoy birds, deer, landscape and tracks too.
  4. EveningDinner + early sleepKeep the next morning free for safari.

DAY 2 · Morning safari + calm exit

  1. Before dawnWake and report on timeCarry ID, permit, water and a light layer. Your hotel should confirm transport the previous night.
  2. MorningSafari drive 2Use this as a fresh forest experience, not a repeat of yesterday's route.
  3. Late morningBreakfast + check outRest before packing or continuing by train/road.
  4. AfternoonDeparture bufferKeep a margin for station transfer; do not book an impossibly tight connection.

PLAN C · THREE DAYS

Safaris, Ranthambore Fort and a buffer

Choose three days when you want a third safari opportunity or time for the fort without squeezing it between permit slots. Fort access and weather should be checked locally before you go.

3 daysBest relaxed plan
DAY 1

Arrival + safari

Reach Sawai Madhopur, check your permit details and take a confirmed afternoon safari.

DAY 2

Morning safari + rest

Enjoy your second drive. Keep the afternoon light; do not force a hot fort walk after an early safari.

DAY 3

Fort or third safari

Choose Ranthambore Fort only if access and weather suit you, or take a third permit-based safari if available.

Why 3 days is best:Wildlife travel needs buffer. You get time for the forest, a fort option and a departure that is not rushed.

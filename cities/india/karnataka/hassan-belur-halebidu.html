FIRST, CHOOSE YOUR PACE

How much Hoysala time do you really have?

One day is enough for Belur and Halebidu when you start from Hassan. Two days add Shravanabelagola at a respectful pace. There is no need to turn this quiet heritage circuit into a list of every temple in the district.

YOUR READY HERITAGE PLAN

Look closely.
Travel simply.

Hassan district itself runs a Hassan–Belur–Halebidu day circuit from its City Bus Stand, listing 117 km total. The route below keeps the same sensible core but removes the pressure to add every intermediate stop.

PLAN A · ONE FULL DAY

Belur in the morning, Halebidu in the afternoon

Begin from Hassan early. Karnataka Tourism lists Belur’s Chennakeshava Temple from 7:30 AM to 7:30 PM, but early morning gives you the better light and more patient viewing time. Halebidu comes after a real lunch break.

HASSAN → BELUR

Leave early and keep the temple morning intact

The Hassan district’s current organised circuit departs its City Bus Stand at 7:30 AM and reaches Belur at 9:15 AM. Self-drive or taxi timing can vary, so use the route as a calm planning reference rather than a rushed promise.

PLAN B · TWO FULL DAYS

Keep Belur–Halebidu on Day 1; climb Shravanabelagola on Day 2

Day 1 is the core Hoysala route above. Day 2 is for Jain heritage and the Vindhyagiri climb. The official Hassan district tour treats Shravanabelagola as its own 132 km Hassan round trip—do the same.

PLAN C · ONLY WITH EXTRA TIME

Choose one small extension, not another giant circuit.

There is no need to force a third packed day. Use it for a slow temple revisit, a confirmed Yagachi activity, or one smaller Halebidu-side monument—only after Belur, Halebidu and Shravanabelagola are already complete.
